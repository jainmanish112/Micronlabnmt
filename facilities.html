Facilities

Aconity MIDI Lab metal 3D printer

At New Mexico Tech, this laser powder bed fusion system builds metal parts layer by layer from powder. We use it to vary the geometry and composition of titanium and Ti–Cu specimens before depositing and annealing thin films on their surfaces.

MTS Mini Bionix II fatigue tester

The department’s servohydraulic test frame applies repeated loads to bulk specimens. We use cyclic mechanical testing to study damage at the specimen scale. The department also operates an MTS Criterion 43 for complementary mechanical tests.

MEMS thin-film fatigue tester

Through collaboration with Sandia’s Center for Integrated Nanotechnologies (CINT), we use microelectromechanical systems (MEMS) to cycle thin films near 10 kHz. Electron microscopy reveals where grain growth or cracks first appear.

Laser-induced particle impact testing (LIPIT)

This New Mexico Tech setup uses a laser pulse to launch a microparticle toward a target. The impact probes how materials deform under rapid, localized loading.
